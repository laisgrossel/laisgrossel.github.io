Hello! I am a Brazilian evolutionary biologist interested in animal behavior, mainly in sexual and social selection. 
Echoing West-Eberhard's work ([1979](https://www.jstor.org/stable/pdf/986582.pdf), [1983](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2828804)), I believe we might explore sexual selection as a subprocess within social selection, when the resources over which individuals compete are related to reproduction (such as mates, either through choice or direct confrontation). Even so, it is difficult to disentangle these two forms of selection, especially when female preferences for specific male traits favor the selection of parental individuals, for example, and males remain associated with their clutches, providing offspring protection, a form of social behavior. I have studied this interplay during my PhD, exploring the two pre-copulatory mechanisms of sexual selection to understand how parental males still have to invest in [sexual advertisement](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00265-025-03578-4) to females; and how the [availability of reproductive resources](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/eth.13511), such as nests, might influence the costs of care and male-male competition. 
On the other hand, social selection might include non-sexual selection when the disputed resources include food, shelter, or status in hierarchies, a question that I explored during my Master's by investigating how [hierarchies of dominance are reorganized](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10211-021-00386-7) in the absence of high-status individuals.

As a Latina researcher, I plan to build a career by keeping Science **(I) collaborative, diverse and welcoming**, bringing together colleagues with different skills, backgrounds, and perspectives to approach the same problem; **(II) transparent**, strengthening confidence in the knowledge we produce through collective efforts by our [open science societies](https://sortee.org/about/); and **(III) accessible and reliable** through science outreach, being accountable to public for the work produced within universities and (re)building public trust in scientific institutions.
